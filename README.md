## The Slab Does Not Forgive

Concrete sets, and flexibility sets with it. Sleeves, shaft openings, and core cuts are cheap to move while the drawings are still soft, and painfully expensive once reinforcement is tied. A drain line needs its slope, a chilled water riser needs its bend radius, and neither will adjust to suit a ceiling height someone approved last month.

When building services are part of the conversation from concept stage, ceiling voids get sized once: ducts, sprinkler mains, cable trays, and light fittings share a few hundred millimetres, and settling that argument on paper costs nothing. Openings cut later also demand structural certification, which adds both money and calendar time.

## Plant Rooms Are Real Estate

Architects often treat plant areas as leftover space. Chillers, transformers, DG sets, LT panels, and pump rooms each carry clearance, ventilation, and access demands that quietly dictate floor area. Experienced [**MEP Consultants in Delhi NCR**](https://sanelac.com/) push to freeze plant locations before elevations are finalised, because a transformer room sitting ten metres closer to the main panel can remove tens of metres of heavy copper cable.

That is a saving no value-engineering exercise can recover later, since the building has already been shaped around the mistake.

## Honest Loads Before Equipment Selection

Rule-of-thumb sizing remains the most expensive habit in the industry. A chiller specified “just in case” costs more to buy, more to house, and more to run, and it spends most of its life at part load where efficiency suffers. The same logic applies to transformers and standby generators. Projects in 2026 add fresh variables: EV charging points, rooftop solar, server rooms in ordinary offices, and the updated Energy Conservation and Sustainable Building Code.

Early load studies let the design absorb these without oversizing everything, and phased expansion can be planned instead of improvised. Terrace space is another quiet casualty: solar arrays, cooling towers, and condensers compete for one roof, and their loads must be known before the slab is designed.

Three early decisions tend to protect budgets more than any later negotiation:

- Freezing plant room locations and shaft positions before facade design closes
- Running clash checks in a coordinated model before procurement begins
- Sizing electrical and cooling loads on realistic occupancy, with space left for phased growth

## A Familiar Scenario

Picture an office floor where a supply duct must cross a structural beam, a sprinkler main sits directly beneath it, and a cable tray wants the same corridor. Nobody flagged it at design stage, so the false ceiling drops by 150 mm and the architect’s promised height disappears. The remedies on site are all unwelcome: reroute and add bends, resize the duct and lose airflow, or cut into the beam with a structural sign-off. Resolved early, the same crossing is a single line moved on a drawing.

## Approvals and Fire Safety Do Not Wait

Fire detection, sprinkler layouts, smoke extraction, staircase pressurisation, and DG exhaust routing all face authority review. Late services changes can trigger resubmission, and resubmission eats weeks. Fire-rated shafts and pressurisation fans also need structural support and floor area, which makes them poor candidates for last-minute squeezing. Compliance built into the first design set moves through approvals with fewer objections and far fewer redrawn sheets.

## Procurement Gets Sharper

Coordinated drawings produce tighter bills of quantities. Contractors price what they can see, and when unknowns are removed, the risk padding that inflates bids shrinks with them. Variation orders drop because fewer surprises surface mid-build. Long-lead items such as switchgear, chillers, VRF outdoor units, and transformers can be ordered against a stable design, which protects the programme from supply delays. Lifecycle cost improves as well, since most of a building’s real expense sits in operation, and efficient systems chosen early keep paying back long after handover.

## Mistakes Owners Repeat

Appointing on lowest fee alone, treating the consultant as a drafting vendor, and releasing tenant requirements after layouts freeze account for most avoidable overruns. Maintainability is overlooked just as often. Equipment placed where filters, valves, and drive units cannot be reached gets serviced late, fails early, and quietly raises operating cost every year.
